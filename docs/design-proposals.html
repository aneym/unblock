unblock: three design systems

The current page is a single 580px column of stacked article-style cards. It reads fine at three asks and turns into scrolling homework at ten. Each system below reorganizes the queue around a different answer to "how should a human process this pile", now that every ask carries an explicit project. Each is a full swap: tokens, type, and layout.

System 1

Ledger — the ops console

Thesis: the queue is a manifest, not an essay. Show every ask as one dense row, projects as a persistent left rail, and expand exactly one row in place. You triage with your eyes before touching anything, and j/k/enter does the rest.

Type: system sans at 13px for content, SF Mono for every piece of metadata. Radius 6–7px. Spacing on a 4px grid, rows 40px tall. Dark by default; the amber dot is the only thing allowed to glow.

Rail = the project picker, always visible, waiting-dot per project. One row expanded; the other three stay one line each.

Wins when

  • The queue regularly holds 8+ asks across 3+ projects.
  • You answer at a desk with a keyboard, herdr-adjacent.
  • You want the Unblock/Grill tabs gone: purpose becomes a badge, not a page split.

Tradeoffs

  • Worst of the three on a phone; the rail must collapse to a sheet.
  • Dense rows make long titles fight for space.
  • Dark chrome around secret inputs needs care to keep paste targets obvious.
System 2

Deck — one ask at a time

Thesis: stop making the human do the sequencing. The queue already knows what matters: gating first, then oldest. So show exactly one ask, full-width and calm, with the rest stacked underneath. Answer, and the next one slides up. The project picker is a pill in the header; the segmented meter shows how deep the deck is.

#F7F2EA paper #FFFDF9 card #2B2620 ink #B4530A accent #E2D9CB rule

Type: serif display (New York / Charter) for ask titles, sans for everything else, mono only for the progress counter. Radius 12–18px. Warm paper palette; feels like correspondence, not a dashboard. Phone-first.

One card owns the screen. The stacked edges below are the rest of the deck; the meter and "2 of 4" keep the end in sight.

Wins when

  • Most answering happens on the phone from the tailnet URL.
  • Asks deserve real attention: grills, secrets, anything with steps.
  • You want finishing the queue to feel like inbox-zero, with a real end state.

Tradeoffs

  • No scanning: you cannot see ask 4 while answering ask 1.
  • Trusts the sort order completely; a bad ordering is felt immediately.
  • Batch actions (accept-all across asks) do not fit the model.
System 3

Board — the control room

Thesis: the projects are the page. One lane per project, compact cards inside, the whole machine visible at once. You do not pick a project; you look at all of them and answer where the pile is deepest. Cheap decisions get one-tap quick answers right on the card; anything heavier expands in place.

#EEF1F5 bg #E4E8EE lane #1C2333 ink #F59E0B blocker #7C6CF0 decision

Type: one sans throughout at 13px, mono for counts and ages. Radius 9–12px. Cool neutral field so the two status hues carry all the meaning. Lanes stack vertically on narrow screens, which quietly becomes a grouped list.

Every project at once. The amber/violet edge is the blocker/decision split; recommended answers become one-tap buttons.

Wins when

  • Many agents run in parallel and the question is "where is the fleet stuck".
  • Most decisions are ratifications: accept the recommendation and move on.
  • The page doubles as a status wall you leave open on a monitor.

Tradeoffs

  • One-tap accept trades deliberation for speed; must_decide fields need to resist it visibly.
  • Lanes get cramped past 5 projects; needs lane collapse.
  • Deep asks (12 fields, secrets) still need a full expanded view, so it is two designs in one.
Picking

How they differ, in one line each

Ledger optimizes for scanning, Deck for attention, Board for oversight. My read: Deck is the best match for how you actually answer (phone, a few at a time, gating first), with Ledger's project rail worth stealing for the desktop width. Say which one and I will build it into web/; the current tokens all live in styles.css, so a system swap is contained.
